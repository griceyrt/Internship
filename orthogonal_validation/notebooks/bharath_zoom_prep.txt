ZOOM PREP — POSSIBLE QUESTIONS FROM BHARATH
=============================================
Project: Orthogonal Validation | Dataset: GSE130613 | Date: 2026-06-19


==============================================================================
FASTQC / MULTIQC
==============================================================================

Q: What did FastQC show about read quality?
A: All 8 samples PASS per-base sequence quality and per-sequence quality scores.
   Reads are 75bp, 5.7–8.1M reads per sample. Quality is not the issue.

Q: Was my adapter sequence correct?
A: Yes. AGATCGGAAGAGCACACGTCTGAACTCCAGTCA is confirmed in the adapter content
   plot and appears in the top overrepresented sequences table.

Q: What modules failed in FastQC?
A: Per Sequence GC Content (FAIL — all samples), Adapter Content (FAIL — all samples),
   Per Base Sequence Content (WARN, SRR9002585 FAIL), Sequence Duplication (WARN all).
   These are all expected for MARS-Seq 3' end data, not signs of bad sequencing.

Q: Why is duplication so high?
A: 41–49% duplication across samples. Expected for 3' end sequencing — all reads
   come from the same end of the transcript, so you're repeatedly sequencing the
   same region. Not a quality problem.

Q: What are the overrepresented sequences?
A: Mostly variants of the Illumina adapter with slightly different biological
   bases preceding it. Also polyT runs (complement of the mRNA polyA tail) —
   characteristic of MARS-Seq reading through the 3' end.

Q: Why does GC content fail?
A: The GC distribution is not the expected normal bell curve. Likely due to
   MARS-Seq 3' end bias — reads don't sample the transcriptome uniformly.

Q: Did adapter trimming improve the mapping rate?
A: Yes, slightly. SRR9002567: 56.71% → 60.55% after adapter trimming.
   Modest improvement (~4 percentage points).

Q: Did polyA trimming improve it further?
A: Testing in progress on SRR9002567. Early read shows ~61.5% (still running).
   Final number pending.

Q: Why are KO samples mapping lower than WT?
A: Consistent ~10% difference. Could reflect biological differences in transcript
   composition, or technical variability between sample groups.

Q: Why is SRR9002569 so much higher (70%)?
A: Unknown. Could be sample-specific library quality or RNA integrity.

Q: Why are mapping rates low overall?
A: MARS-Seq is 3' end sequencing. Reads only cover the 3' end of transcripts.
   Our index is built from a custom transcriptome (transcriptome_ext.fa) that
   may not cover all 3' ends perfectly. PolyA contamination also reduces mapping
   (polyA runs don't match the transcriptome).


==============================================================================
SALMON SETUP & INDEX
==============================================================================

Q: What mode did you use for Salmon?
A: Quasi-mapping (not alignment-based). Single-end mode (-r flag) because
   MARS-Seq is single-end.

Q: What library type?
A: -l A (auto-detect). Salmon detected -l U (unstranded).
   Confirmed correct: compatible_fragment_ratio = 1.0, strand_mapping_bias = 0.0.

Q: What transcriptome did you use for the index?
A: Khushi's custom transcriptome: transcriptome_ext.fa (mm39-based).
   Built gentrome_ext.fa (transcriptome + mm39 genome as decoy) and decoys.txt.
   Index flags: -k 31 --keepDuplicates

Q: What genome version?
A: mm39 (GRCm39). Khushi confirmed her transcriptome is also mm39 — no version
   mismatch.

Q: What Salmon version?
A: Salmon 2.0.0


==============================================================================
DATASET
==============================================================================

Q: What dataset did you use?
A: GSE130613 (Manella & Asher, PNAS 2020). SRP accession: SRP194523.
   Liver WT vs Per1/2 DKO, constant darkness, CT16-20, n=4 per group.

Q: Why this dataset and not GSE171975?
A: Both are MARS-Seq 3' end. You approved switching to GSE130613 because it has
   n=4 replicates vs n=3, giving slightly better statistical power.

Q: How did you download the data?
A: prefetch + fasterq-dump (sra-tools). 8 samples, single-end FASTQ files.

Q: Did the original paper use Salmon?
A: No. Original authors used STAR + HTSeq-count on mm10. We reprocessed the raw
   FASTQs with Salmon on mm39 using the custom transcriptome.


==============================================================================
NORMALISATION & DESEQ2
==============================================================================

Q: How did you normalize the data?
A: Raw counts from tximport → filter (>5 counts in ≥2 samples: 155,263 → 20,754
   transcripts) → divide by total reads per sample. Simple library-size
   normalization. This table feeds SUPPA.

Q: Did you do batch correction?
A: No. No batch information is available for the GSE130613 DD samples.

Q: What DESeq2 thresholds did you use?
A: |log2FC| > 0.5 AND padj < 0.05. Kiran's decision (relaxed from 1.5).
   343 significant DE genes.

Q: Why padj and not p-value for DESeq2?
A: padj = p-value adjusted for multiple testing (Benjamini-Hochberg). With
   thousands of genes tested simultaneously, raw p-values produce many false
   positives. padj controls the false discovery rate.

Q: What is tximport?
A: R package that reads Salmon quant.sf files into R and collapses transcript-
   level counts to gene level using a tx2gene table (built from
   transcriptome_productivity.gtf).


==============================================================================
SUPPA / SPLICING
==============================================================================

Q: What significance thresholds did you use for splicing?
A: Illumina (this project): |dPSI| > 0.1 AND p < 0.3
   Nanopore (Khushi's):     |dPSI| > 0.1 AND p < 0.05

Q: Why p < 0.3 for Illumina splicing?
A: Low power: n=4 replicates with the empirical bootstrapping method in SUPPA.
   Strict 0.05 cutoff left almost nothing. p < 0.3 is a known compromise for
   small n empirical tests. Documented as a limitation.

Q: What SUPPA boundary setting?
A: -b S (strict). Confirmed by Khushi and the manuscript methods.

Q: What diffSplice method?
A: Empirical (--method empirical), with -gc flag for cluster generation.

Q: What events did you test?
A: SE (exon skipping), SS (alternative splice sites), MX (mutually exclusive
   exons), RI (intron retention), FL (first/last exon). All standard SUPPA event
   types.

Q: What were the final splicing results?
A: 532 significant Illumina splicing events (457 unique genes)
   971 significant Nanopore splicing events
   32 overlapping events (31 unique genes) — orthogonal validation
   At strict p<0.05 in both: Cpeb4, Mup2, 0610005C13Rik

Q: What is PSI?
A: Percent Spliced In. For a given splicing event, it's the fraction of
   transcripts that include the alternative exon/sequence. Range 0–1.
   dPSI = difference in PSI between conditions (we show KO - WT).

Q: What is dPSI sign convention?
A: SUPPA output = WT - KO. We negate it in plots to show KO - WT.
   Positive dPSI = more inclusion in KO.
